Breaking the 7-Bit BF16 Mantissa Ceiling: O(εbf162/3) Residual-Rescaled Spectral Volterra–Newton Refinement and High-Noise Desingularization for Continuous-Time Generative Flows

Andrew Rober Fouad Samy · BytesGlue (Dubai, United Arab Emirates) · andrew.rober@bytesglue.com

Abstract

Modern AI accelerators achieve their highest arithmetic throughput and lowest memory-bandwidth pressure in BFLOAT16 (BF16), whose 7-bit significand imposes a coarse unit roundoff εbf16 = 2−8. Consequently, standard sequential and time-parallel continuous-time generative solvers—including DDIM, DPM-Solver-2, and sliding-window Picard iteration (ParaDiGMS)—hit a permanent 7-bit fixed-point stagnation wall once trajectory corrections fall below 2−7||X|| (~2.1×10−3 relative L2 error; 38–46 dB PSNR). On pretrained variance-preserving diffusion models, dividing BF16 noise predictions by αT ≈ 6.57×10−3 at t=T further amplifies quantization noise by 152.2×. We introduce Central-Difference Scaled-Perturbation BF16 Iterative Refinement (CSP-BF16-IR), an intralayer-free mixed-precision spectral Volterra–Newton framework that recovers strict 24-bit IEEE-FP32 trajectory precision while executing all dense trajectory and Jacobian sweeps on unmodified 7-bit BF16 Tensor Cores. Dynamically upscaling sparse FP32 Clenshaw–Curtis Volterra defect vectors Rk via antipodal pairs Xk ± scRk (σc = 2−3 = 0.125) cancels the even Hessian identically within a single fused [2M, d] BF16 Tensor-Core batch, improving directional JVP error from O(ε1/2) to O(ε2/3) (3.68× single-sweep gain). Combined with root-time z = √τ high-noise desingularization and raw PTX + CUDAGraph execution on an NVIDIA GB10 Grace Blackwell Superchip (aarch64, CUDA 13.0) and NVIDIA RTX 5060 (x86_64), CSP-BF16-IR reaches 9.37×10−8 L2 error on 46.91M SwiGLU DiTs (44.2× faster than 512-NFE CUDAGraph FP32 RK4; 170× in ParaFlow-BF16), beats ParaDiGMS in both speed and +16.2 dB PSNR on 2D flow matching, and shatters the BF16 ceiling on official google/ddpm-cifar10-32 (69.69 dB; 86.08 dB vs. exact FP32 collocation) and 64×-larger 256×256 google/ddpm-celebahq-256 (113.67M UNet, +24.45 dB gain to 50.10 dB, surpassing 100-NFE FP32 DPM-Solver-2 by +3.90 dB) with zero hyperparameter retuning.
